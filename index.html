Finding MP6

After you enter the ARC, take the hallway to the right. MP6 is at the end of the hallway, on your right. Signs along the walls point the way.

How open play works

There are 8 tables for open play. The basic rules:

  • Warm up for 2 minutes at most.
  • Both players rotate off after each game.
  • To queue, put both players' paddles next to the table.
  • Queue for one table at a time.
  • Don't save a spot for someone who isn't there.

Challenge tables

Two tables are staffed by competitive team members. Anyone can challenge them, whatever your skill level.

Beginner tips

  • You don't need to be good. Plenty of regulars started the same way you are.
  • Rally before you race. Keeping the ball going teaches more than trying to win points.
  • Watch the table rotation. Members take turns. Watch a minute, then ask who is next.
  • Ask anything. Questions are normal and welcome here.

Equipment

Don't buy anything yet.

Need a paddle for your first practice? The ARC has loaners, though they are fairly basic, and the club has a limited number too. You can also borrow one from a friend or a member. If enough people want their own paddle, the club posts a poll in the Discord equipment channel and can coordinate a bulk order for a better price.

Marvin answers equipment questions, and the picks below are his.

An easy first paddle

For casual beginners on a budget, a pre-assembled paddle is fine:

  • Palio Expert: his general top pick for beginners
  • Yinhe 7B: the cheaper option
Going more serious? Build a custom paddle

Pre-made vs custom. Pre-made paddles are mass-produced, so the blade and rubbers are usually lower quality, which can limit some techniques. The two above are good exceptions. Most dedicated players buy the blade and rubbers separately so the paddle fits their style.

Blade. Pick a 5-ply all-wood blade:

  • Donic Persson Power Allround
  • Stiga Allround Classic
  • Butterfly Petr Korbel
  • Tibhar Stratus Power Wood
  • Butterfly Primorac
  • Stiga Clipper Classic

Rubbers. Pair it with two slower, controllable rubbers, with a sponge thickness of 2.0 at most. Get one in black and the other in red or another color.

  • Xiom Vega Intro: his overall top pick
  • Butterfly Sriver
  • Yasaka Mark V
  • Donic Baracuda
  • Andro Hexer Grip (comes in green)
  • Donic Bluefire M1
  • Yasaka Rakza 7 (better for forehand) or Rakza 7 Soft (better for backhand)

Want the details on sponge thickness and hardness? Read this in-depth guide to rubbers.

Assembly. Paddle Palace and Table Tennis 11 assemble it for free if you buy the whole setup from them. Marvin also offers an assembly service if that doesn't work for you. Advanced beginners and intermediate players who want to upgrade can message him for recommendations.

Tryouts

Only if you want to compete.

Tryouts are for the club's competitive teams. They are held in MP6 during regular practice, and the details are posted in the club Discord. In fall 2026 they ran on Tuesday, Sept 29 and Wednesday, Sept 30.

  • Teams: Coed (A team of 8, B team of 4) and Women's (8 players). Women can try out for one or both.
  • Dues first: you must have paid dues to take part.
  • Sign-up: through a Google form posted before tryouts. No walk-ins.
  • Format: matches are best of 3 sets. In fall 2026, coed tryouts ran over both days and women's trials were on the second day.

Joined after tryouts? Chances to join a team are handled case by case. Tell a board member you are still interested.

Regular practices are open to every member whether or not you try out.

Table tennis lingo

Sound like you've been here a while.

Rally

The ball going back and forth. Also what you ask for when you want to hit.

Serve

The shot that starts each point. It must bounce on your side, then theirs.

Let

A serve that clips the net and lands on the right side. Replay it.

Loop

A topspin drive: the ball arcs up and dips fast.

Chop

A defensive backspin shot that keeps the ball low and slow.

Shakehand

The most common grip. Hold the paddle as if shaking hands with it.

Quick answers

Tap a question.

Do I need to fill out a form to join?

No. Paying dues makes you a member.

Do I need experience?

No. Beginners are welcome.

Do I have to bring my own paddle?

No. The ARC has loaner paddles, though they are fairly basic, and the club has a limited number too. See Equipment.

Can I come before I pay?

Yes. New members get 4 free trial practices at the start of each semester. After that, pay your $25 dues to keep playing.

Do I have to try out to play?

No. Tryouts are only for the Coed and Women's competitive teams. Everyone can play open play.

Can I play against the competitive team?

Yes. Two challenge tables have competitive team members who play anyone, at any skill level.
